# AI-assisted review, 22 October 2026 – client deletion and replay

Not an independent audit. Three separate runs of an AI model (Claude), each given the core at
`914288a` (0.11.0) together with the app that pins it (Dôverník), reviewed two questions; the
maintainer re-verified the findings against the code. Written down so that the formal auditor can
treat the core-side findings as known. The application findings are tracked in the app's own
repository and are not detailed here.

**Scope**

1. Deleting a client and destroying its keys (crypto-shredding): the local database, local backups
   and backup chunks, mirrors, cloud storage.
2. Replay and rollback: can a server, a storage location or an older backup return a genuine but
   outdated sealed record, wrapped key or state, and would the app notice?

**Outcome.** Most findings were in the application – how long it keeps old wraps of its master key,
what "delete from this computer" removes, mirrors that do not receive a deletion, and freshness
fields missing from its own sync payload – all outside this repository. Two findings touched the
core:

| # | Severity | Where | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | `vault/src/lib.rs` `Vault::open` | SQLCipher ran without `secure_delete` and the apps never ran `VACUUM`: deleted rows (names, contacts, dates stored as plain columns) stayed in the free space of the pages and in old WAL frames – readable by whoever has the database key, e.g. after a later compromise of the OS keystore | **fixed** in 0.11.1: `PRAGMA secure_delete = ON` on every open; `Vault::compact()` (checkpoint and truncate the WAL, `VACUUM`, truncate again) for the app to call after deleting sensitive data, `Error::Busy` when another connection blocks the checkpoint. Tests read the setting back, check an empty WAL and a smaller file, and show on a plaintext file that the deleted text is gone while a control run without the setting keeps it |
| 2 | Informational | `AUDIT.md`, `ts/src/sync.ts`, README | The documentation said only that swapped blobs are rejected; it did not say that freshness, order, protection against replay or rollback and tombstones are not provided by the core and must come from the app | **fixed** in 0.11.1: AUDIT.md section 3.3 and two threat-model rows, the `sync.ts` header and the README say what the app must do (version and prev-hash chain in the sealed payload, rotation epoch, per-device high-water mark, tombstones) |

Open questions passed on to the formal auditor (also in AUDIT.md section 3.3 and question 7 + 12):

- Should the core offer an optional freshness envelope (sequence, epoch and previous-state hash
  bound in the AAD) so that each app does not build its own?
- Is `secure_delete` plus `compact()` after sensitive deletions sufficient for a database whose
  pages are encrypted, given that `cipher_memory_security` stays off?
